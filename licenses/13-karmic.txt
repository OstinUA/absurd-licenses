3. Corrective Obligation.

Upon discovering a Bug, the Recipient shall prepare and submit a
Corrective Pull Request within seven calendar days. Opening an Issue,
posting a complaint, adding a reaction, or writing "TODO: someone fix"
does not satisfy the obligation. A repair confined permanently to the
Recipient's private branch remains an undistributed improvement and
does not restore the shared condition.

4. Maintainer Independence.

The obligation is satisfied by submission of a technically serious
Corrective Pull Request; acceptance, merger, and the maintainer's mood
are outside the Recipient's responsibility. If submission to the original
repository is unavailable, a publicly accessible Pull Request against
the Recipient's fork qualifies, provided it identifies the original
work and makes the proposed repair available for incorporation.

5. Attachment of Karmic Consequences.

Each Discovered Bug remaining without a Corrective Pull Request after
the seven-day period creates one outstanding karmic imbalance. Such an
imbalance subjects the Recipient to Minor Adversity at intervals not
disclosed by the Author. No particular spill is guaranteed, but repeated
claims that the Bug is "someone else's problem" may be considered
administratively relevant by the universe.

6. Nature of Enforcement.

Minor Adversity is cumulative, nontransferable, and not redeemable with
repository stars. The Recipient shall not assign the imbalance to an
intern, a corporate subsidiary, or a houseplant. A failure to establish
the causal connection between a Bug and a missing key does not establish
that the key was ever going to be easy to find.

7. Cure and Records.

Late submission of a Corrective Pull Request discharges the relevant

experienced inconvenience. A Pull Request consisting solely of cosmetic
changes does not cure an unrelated functional Bug. Distributors shall
preserve this License; each Recipient is responsible only for Bugs they
actually discover, not for acquiring omniscient test coverage.

8. Continuing Effect.
